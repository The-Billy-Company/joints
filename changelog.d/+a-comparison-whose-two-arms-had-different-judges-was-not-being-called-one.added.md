`--against` and `--twice` refuse two boards read from different source trees.
They said nothing about two boards judged by different oracles, which for
`square` — the one number on this project that is a claim about agreement with a
second parser — is the same defect on the controlled variable that matters most.

`still.judged` is that refusal, and it took the same care about which differences
are fatal, because a gate that fires on every honest before/after is a gate
somebody passes with a flag. Fatal, in the order they are checked: the identity
**rule** the two digests obey, before anything else; the **CLI**, once rather
than thirty times, since one `tree-sitter generate` lowered all of them; the
**authored identity** of any grammar both arms consulted; and a **torn tree** —
one identity with two generated parsers, which means at least one arm was
answered by a `parser.c` that was not produced from the `grammar.json` beside it.
`oracle_build` regenerates only when the grammar digests differ, so that state is
reachable and the identity is designed not to see it; two digests answer both
questions and neither answers the other's.

Not fatal: a grammar one arm consulted and the other did not (the shared set is
still a comparison), and one arm having asked where the other only cited (fewer
fields, like `unrecorded`). Silent: the seat, the library path, the library's
bytes, the pin tag. None can change a verdict, and `pin.py arm` hands every arm
its own seat *by design*, so a gate that read them would refuse every honest
before/after on this machine. They are silent because the witness does not record
them at all, which is a stronger guarantee than remembering not to compare them.

The oracle is deliberately not demoted the way `subject` is when two runs share
one binary. One binary means the source tree could not have moved a number; it
does not mean the oracle could not, because `crooked` comes out of a cache whose
rows are accepted only while the identity that produced them holds.

Proved on real witnesses rather than typed ones: one authored byte flipped in a
copy of css's scanner, taken outside the shared tree, and the pair refuses
naming css and its two digests.
