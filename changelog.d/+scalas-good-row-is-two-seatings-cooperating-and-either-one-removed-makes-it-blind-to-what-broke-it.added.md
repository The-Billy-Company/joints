Scala carries two of today's fourteen `Troupe` rows, and its 79.4% standing is
produced by the pair rather than by either. An uncommitted `src/press/`
intermediate - two files, `bench.zig` and `ladder.zig`, alive in the tree for about
ten minutes and since withdrawn by the lane that owns them - moved scala's damage
4,150 → 16,883 and its standing to 16.03%, and elixir's 0 → 8,795 from a perfect
row to 80.92%. Twenty-four grammars stayed byte-identical in all 31 columns; verilog
and sql improved, then went back when the delta did.

The regression is gone. What it exposed is not:

| scala's state | before the press files | during |
|---|---|---|
| both today-seated rows in | 4,150 | **16,883** |
| `_indent/.offside/.slashes` removed | 11,913 | 11,913 |
| `block_comment/.marrow/.kotlin_block` removed | 6,557 | 6,557 |

With either row absent scala is insensitive to the press edit **to the byte**. Only
with both present does it move at all. So a press change that disturbs either half
of that cooperation costs the whole 12,733 bytes at once, and **no arm that ablates
one row at a time can see it** - both of scala's own isolation arms report the
regression as zero. Neither could a folio diff, for the usual reason.

Two things follow. A grammar whose standing depends on two seatings interacting is
a grammar whose next regression is attributable to neither of them, and scala is now
the known instance. And a board taken once on a tree ten agents are editing prices a
moment rather than a change: two controls four minutes apart in the same audit
disagreed by 63 standing points on one grammar for reasons belonging to neither arm,
which is why the fourteen arms were re-anchored to a single pinned snapshot and
`--mine` was pointed at that snapshot instead of at the live tree.
