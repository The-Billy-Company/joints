When a declared conflict splits the parse and every reading but one dies,
`roost` collapses the survivor back to the flat lone discipline: perch chain to
array indices, runs re-tiled bottom-up in `borne`. It did that by rebuilding
the entire stack from the ground, every time - and the 129 KB cpp corpus
splits 5,015 times, so a conflict refuted three tokens after it opened repaid
the file's whole stack in copying, thousands of times over.

A `keel` watermark now pins the lowest perch the divergence can reach. Below
it every reading's chain is the same flat prefix laid down under the lone
discipline - the indices are the chain, the runs already tile - so the
survivor's walk stops where it re-enters the prefix and only the tail above
the keel is rebuilt. `absorb` raises the keel to the stack top when a lone
parse splits; a forked fold that pops past it lowers it to match, so the
prefix claim is maintained rather than assumed.

Worth 77.0 ns/byte from 82.3 on the cpp corpus. The suite's fork/roost tests
hold the collapsed stack byte-identical to the full rebuild.
