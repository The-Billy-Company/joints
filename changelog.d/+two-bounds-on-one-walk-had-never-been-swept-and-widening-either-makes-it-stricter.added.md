`climb` and `chase` bound the same lookahead walk - pretend-perches and steps -
and a step is what stacks a perch, so `chase` under `climb` makes `climb`
unreachable. That is `crowd`/`skeins`' wiring, and neither number had ever been
measured; they were hoisted out of `shiftable` as locals and kept their values.

Swept jointly, seven arms on one tree, every board against the frozen `limb`
oracle, scored on square without verilog: 1/2 loses **143,879** - php, scala,
kotlin, swift, typescript and julia all collapse - 2/8 loses 23,878, and 4/16,
8/32, 32/32, 8/128 and 32/128 are byte-identical at 309,356 on all thirty
grammars.

The direction is the part worth writing down, because it is backwards from the
pair that prompted the audit. Running out of budget is an *answer* here, not a
pause: `shiftable` says yes when it gives up, so a wider budget is a **stricter**
walk - more chances to reach one of the three refusing arms before the permissive
exit. Raising one of these is not obviously safe, which is why the cliff is
below the current values and the flat is above. 32/128 costs 0.995x the corpus's
parse time and buys nothing.
