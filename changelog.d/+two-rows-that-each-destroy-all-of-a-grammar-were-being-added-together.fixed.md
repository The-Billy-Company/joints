Five two-row subsets were priced and two were called **cooperating** - kotlin at
a −20,288 residual and scala at +5,500. Sighted, **no pair on this board is two
rows cooperating.** Every `square` residual is zero or positive, and a positive
residual is the arithmetic signature of a **ceiling**: two rows that each destroy
nearly all of one quantity cannot also sum.

**elixir is the new one, and it is the one `damage` cannot see at all.** Each of
its two rows *alone* costs 23,878 of elixir's 23,879 square - 99.996% - so the
whole ceiling is the residual. On `damage` the same pair reads 1,472 bytes apart
on a 46,089-byte file, which any slack rule files as additive. A pair sweep run
on `damage` would have called elixir independent and never learned that either
row alone is total.
