A sibling lane is building a sweep that proves every reported field can take more
than one value. On `rack`'s twenty-eight columns over the thirty-row corpus,
**twenty-six clear that bar** - and the bar is the wrong one.

`research/joinery/unjudged/liveness.py` prints a second number beside it:
**`firing`, how many rows the column is non-zero on.** It reuses
`rederive.brood` unchanged - a pure `bytes -> list[(tag, bytes)]`, so pointing
the mutant generator at a second instrument costs a temp file and a
`plumb.Case`, which is the whole claim about cost.

| column | corpus | mutants | |
|---|---|---|---|
| `unjudged` | **2 of 30** | 146 of 390 | 73× the evidence |
| `mute` | **2 of 30** | 106 of 390 | 53× |
| `unwindowed` | **3 of 30** | 49 of 390 | 16× |
| `renamed` | **0 of 30** | **0 of 390** | dead on both |
| `shelter` | **0 of 30** | **0 of 390** | dead on both, by construction |

`unjudged` clears "more than one value" with three distinct numbers and stands on
**two rows** - verilog and sql, both of them tree-sitter's own `ERROR` subtrees.
The manufactured population multiplies the evidence under exactly the columns the
corpus starves and under no others.

Two columns are dead for opposite reasons, and the difference is the part worth
keeping. **`renamed`** - the alias-excusal column - is 0 on every real *and*
manufactured row; its only exercise is a synthetic tripwire, and a mutant cannot
wake it because what it needs is a grammar that aliases something the corpus file
happens to use. **`shelter`** is 0 by construction and must stay 0; it is a
tripwire column, and `verify --price=sheltered` moves it to 1,486 immediately.

So: **a population is not only a set of inputs.** `brood` manufactures inputs;
`--price` and `--oracle=<tag>` manufacture rules, and a column decided by a rule
cannot be woken by any amount of text. A sweep that only varies the corpus will
report `shelter` dead forever, be right about the number, and be wrong about the
column.

Named as worth the same treatment, in the order the evidence says: `plumb`'s own
`unjudged`/`ERROR` arms (`rack`'s denominator, same two rows), `standing.py`'s
`spoil` and `rubble` (the corpus throws almost nothing away), and `attest`'s
stale/absent arms (nobody's corpus is ever missing an artifact).
