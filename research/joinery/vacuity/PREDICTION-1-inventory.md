Written before a single measurement in this lane. The question is not whether
today's seatings work - their own rows are separately evidenced - but whether
the sentence each of them attached, some version of *and no other grammar
moved*, was taken with an instrument that could have said otherwise.

The gate is `still.py::vacuous`, landed at 20:49: when two arms' binaries differ
and **every** item of a kind is byte-identical, that kind is `vacuous` and the
run exits 1. Its own restoration harness reports it firing on rows 1, 3 and 5 of
the recorded six, which is the claim I am here to second.

## The two numbers, and they are different numbers

**Vacuous - the clearance could not have responded: I predict 3 of 10.**

Reasoning, and it is the reason the gap below is the interesting part: a
*folio* comparison is non-responsive to every one of these ten. A *board* or
*tree* comparison is responsive to all ten, because a scanner lives in the
binary and the derivation is recomputed. So the count of vacuous clearances is
just the count of lanes that reached for the folio, and the honest prior is that
most lanes reached for the board because the board is what their own headline
number came from. Three is the gate's own count on the recorded six and I have
no reason to expect the wider population is worse.

**Actually wrong - a grammar really moved and nobody saw: I predict 0.**

The gap between 3 and 0 is the point. A vacuous clearance is an unsupported
claim, not a false one, and the base rate for these seatings is favourable: a
`Troupe` member is admitted only where its roster names a permission, so a
seating's blast radius is bounded by the grammars sharing that vein. Two
mechanisms make me hold a little probability back rather than predict 0 with
confidence:

1. **Shared veins.** `marrow/kotlin_block` "once read `{kotlin, scala}` and let
   either widen onto the other silently" - a vein whose guest list grows is the
   one way a seating reaches a grammar nobody was measuring.
2. **`built` falling while `unbound` falls.** L1 records kotlin's `built`
   dropping 1,075 bytes and calls it a trap rather than a regression. It was
   accepted on an argument, not cleared by a control.

So: 0 predicted, and if I am wrong it is L1 or a shared vein, in that order.

**A third number, which I expect to dominate both: 4 of 10 have arms that
cannot bear the fourth house rule.** `pin.py` landed at 14:40 and the
shared-cache defect (`order.miss`) was not named until 19:31. L1, L2, L3 and L4
were all measured before one or both existed. Their instruments were responsive
in kind; the question is whether their two arms differed by only their own rows,
and for a pair taken 27 minutes apart on a tree ten lanes are editing the prior
is that they did not.

## What kills each prediction

- **Vacuous ≠ 3.** Any count is a finding; the falsifier is my *reasoning*, not
  the number. If I find a lane whose only collateral evidence was a folio diff
  and I have called it cleared, the sort is wrong and the sort is the lane.
- **Actually wrong ≠ 0.** One grammar moving against an isolation arm that no
  contemporaneous instrument named. That is the finding of the week and I would
  rather lose the prediction.
- **The re-measurement is itself vacuous.** If I re-run a pair and every
  grammar including the subject is byte-identical, I have reproduced the shape I
  am auditing one level up, and the honest reading is that the surviving pin
  binaries do not differ in the way the dossier says they do.

## How I will measure, and what it costs

Every landing today was pinned, and 76 pins survive under `.local/pin` with
their binaries and their per-file tree manifests. So the arms are recoverable
without rebuilding: `still against A B --mine <files>` re-judges a recorded pair
against **today's** rules, including vacuity and the `--mine` predicate, and
`standing.py --against` re-takes both boards under separate `JOINTS_WORK` and
`JOINTS_LANE`.

That is a reconstruction, not a live isolation arm, and it costs exactly one
thing: the gap between a pair's two pins still contains whatever the siblings
landed in it. A **clean** re-measurement is therefore strong evidence (no
collateral, and none of the siblings' either), and a **dirty** one is only a
lead - it names files, and attribution then needs an isolation arm built from
today's tree with that landing's rows removed.

I predict I will need to build at most two isolation arms, and that both will be
for landings whose recorded pair comes back dirty for a reason the manifest
already explains.
